LESSONS
The School of Hard Knocks
WHAT EACH
CHAPTER TAUGHT ME.

No classroom hands you these. You earn them by burning food, breaking builds, and writing campaigns that flopped. Here's the unfiltered download.

👨‍🍳 Chapter 01 — Culinary
What The Kitchen Taught Me
01

Presentation is 50% of the product. A dish can taste identical but sell completely differently based on how it looks on the plate. I learned this before I ever heard the word "branding."

02

Speed under pressure is a skill, not a personality trait. Lunch rush doesn't care about your mood. You learn to make good decisions fast — or you hold everyone back.

03

Read the room, always. A great chef adjusts mid-service based on feedback — even unspoken feedback. That's audience intelligence. I just didn't call it that yet.

04

Consistency beats creativity on most days. The special of the day gets attention. The reliable staple builds loyalty. Both matter. Know which one you're serving.

Culinary Arts · Mumbai
💻 Chapter 02 — Software
What The Code Taught Me
01

Understand the system before you try to change it. The worst bugs come from people who skipped the docs. The worst campaigns come from people who skipped the product.

02

Break things on purpose. The fastest way to truly understand something is to try to break it. In code and in campaigns — stress-test your assumptions before the market does.

03

User behaviour is the only truth. What people say they want and what they actually click on are two completely different things. I learned to trust data over opinions early.

04

Complex problems need simple solutions. The best code is readable. The best marketing is the same — if you can't explain it simply, the idea isn't ready yet.

Software Development · Self-taught
📢 Chapter 03 — B2B Marketing · Current
What B2B Taught Me So Far
01

B2B buyers are humans first. They have egos, anxieties, and Slack notifications. Nobody signs a contract because of a feature list. They sign because they trust you.

02

Boring content is a choice, not a category. The "B2B is dry" excuse is just laziness dressed up as professionalism. Every product has a human story. Finding it is the whole job.

03

The brief is almost never the real problem. Someone asks for a brochure. What they actually need is a message that converts. Asking why before asking how has saved more campaigns than any tactic.

04

Still learning. Loudly. This chapter is live. Every campaign is feedback. Every failure is a lesson filed. The edge is in not pretending you've got it figured out.
